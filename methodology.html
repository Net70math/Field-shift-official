Transparency / Data sources

Data & Methodology

Understand where Field Shift's environmental information comes from, how it is processed, and what it can and cannot tell you about the conditions around a farm.

Primary data provider

NASA POWER API

Field Shift uses NASA's Prediction Of Worldwide Energy Resources (POWER) data service as a source of meteorological and solar information. NASA POWER makes globally available, analysis ready environmental data accessible to applications, researchers, and the agricultural community.

NASA
POWER

What kind of data is available?

NASA POWER provides a range of solar and meteorological parameters. The exact variables shown in Field Shift depend on the parameters requested by the app.

☀

Solar radiation

Solar energy reaching the surface, useful for understanding sunlight conditions and potential evaporation or crop-energy context.

温

Air temperature

Near-surface air temperature, including available average, minimum, and maximum measures.

☂

Precipitation

Precipitation estimates that help describe rainfall patterns and support monitoring of wet and dry conditions.

◌

Humidity

Available moisture-related atmospheric measures, including relative humidity and dew-point information.

≋

Wind conditions

Wind speed and direction information at supported heights, useful for describing local atmospheric conditions.

◷

Historical records

Time-series and climatological data help put recent conditions into a longer term context.

How Field Shift uses the data

A simple overview of the intended data workflow.

01

Choose a farm location

The farmer selects a point on the map or allows the device to share its location. The coordinates identify the area for the data request.

02

Request environmental data

The application sends the selected coordinates, requested parameters, and time period to the NASA POWER API.

03

Interpret the returned values

Field Shift organizes the API response and can calculate or classify indicators using documented application rules.

04

Present useful insights

Environmental information is presented in a clearer format to help farmers understand conditions and consider their next steps.

Understanding the data

Important: environmental data is not a field sensor reading

NASA POWER data is derived from satellite observations, models, and other scientific data products. It represents conditions at the source product's spatial resolution, not necessarily the exact conditions at an individual plant or within a small farm plot. Local terrain, elevation, irrigation, shade, and microclimates can cause conditions on a farm to differ from gridded estimates.

Time coverage and update timing

NASA POWER offers hourly, daily, monthly, and climatological products, depending on the service and parameter. NASA's documentation reports that meteorological products are generally available with a latency of around 2–3 days, while solar products are generally around 5–7 days. Therefore, these datasets should not automatically be treated as live, minute-by-minute weather observations or as a local weather forecast.
